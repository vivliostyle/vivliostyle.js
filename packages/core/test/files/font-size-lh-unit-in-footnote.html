The footnote at the page bottom must have the same letter-spacing as the inline "Wide" above: 1lh is the 40px line height of the paragraph, not the line height of the root. For the paragraph with the percentage computed line height it is the 24px of `calc(150%)`, for the paragraph whose ancestor declares `line-height: 1lh` it is the 30px of that ancestor, and for the paragraph with `line-height: min(40px, 2em)` it is the 32px that the math function computes. The paragraphs with `line-height: max(1lh, 5px)`, `line-height: round(1lh, 7px)` and with `line-height: 2lh` on the footnote itself (the grey "Wide" boxes) have a line height of 40px, 42px and 80px, the line height of their source parent and the results of the functions there, and the paragraph with `line-height: round(40px, 7px)` is 42px, so the footnote with `font-size: 1lh` is 42px as well. The paragraph whose own font size is `math` (which only the browser resolves) has an absolute line height of 40px, so the footnote with `font-size: 1lh` is 40px as well. The paragraph whose line height computes to NaN is invalid for this engine, so the 42px that it inherits from the root are the basis of the `lh` unit of its footnote (42px; the browsers censor the NaN result of the declaration itself to 0px), the paragraph with `line-height: min(-10px, -20px)` has the 0px that the non-negative range clamps the function to (so its footnote with `calc(1lh + 10px)` is 10px), the paragraph with `line-height: min(1, 2)` has the 16px of the number 1, and the paragraph with the internal viewport unit `line-height: 2pvw` has the 12.094px of 2% of the page box. The percentage line heights (150% and `calc(150%)`) declared above a level with the larger font size of 32px are the 24px of the 16px font size of the level that declares them, not the 48px of the level below, so their footnotes with `font-size: 1lh` are 24px as well, and the `5ch` of such a level is the assumed 40px (0.5em of the 16px, the metric assumption of CSS Values 4), not the 80px of the level below.
